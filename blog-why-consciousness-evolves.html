Why consciousness evolves and why it must

2026-05-30 · philosophy · consciousness

There's a question worth sitting with: does consciousness have a direction? Not whether brains get more complex, or whether civilisations accumulate more technology, but whether the thing underneath all of it, awareness itself, is going somewhere. I think it is. And I think once you see the mechanism, the evolution stops looking like a hope and starts looking like something closer to a law.

Start with the collective

The first move is to stop thinking of consciousness as a private possession. It's tempting to imagine awareness as a thing locked inside a single skull, separate from every other awareness, walled off. But you can just as easily look at it the other way: as one thing, distributed. Call it a collective. Call it God. Call it the field. The name matters less than the shift in framing, because once you see it as one system rather than billions of isolated ones, the way it changes over time starts to make sense.

And a system like that has a tendency. It moves toward something. The claim here is that what it moves toward is truth, or clarity, or coherence, and that the way it gets there is through experience. Not abstract experience, but lived, direct contact with the world as it actually is.

The problem of baggage

Here's the catch. You cannot experience the world purely if you're carrying baggage.

By baggage I mean the accumulated residue of unresolved trauma, the fears and the wants that we drag through every situation. And fear and want are not separate things from baggage, they're what baggage looks like when it's projected outward. When you walk into a room afraid, you're not perceiving the room, you're perceiving your fear painted onto the room. When you want something badly, you're not seeing the situation, you're seeing your want. Either way, the raw signal of what's actually happening gets buried.

So baggage is noise. It's static laid over the signal. And the more of it you carry, the less of the world you can actually perceive, which means the worse your decisions get, because you're making them from inside the distortion rather than from clear sight.

This is the part that gets misread, so it's worth being precise: experience alone does not clear the baggage. You can rack up a lifetime of raw experience and just keep stacking new distortions on top of the old ones. Experience is the medium, not the mechanism. Something else has to do the actual clearing.

The signal, and the two dependencies

Imagine you're inside a system, a subset of some larger simulation, and there's a signal running through it. The signal is the pull toward clarity. As your baggage reduces, the signal comes through cleaner, and something interesting happens. It doesn't just move you forward, it amplifies itself. Less noise lets more signal through, more signal reduces more noise. The clearing accelerates the clearing.

And the signal does two things at once.

Think of getting anywhere as having two dependencies. First, where do I actually want to go? Second, what do I need to do to get there? When you're buried in baggage, both of these are equally distorted. You don't know what you genuinely want, because fear and want are clouding the destination. And you don't know how to move, because you're navigating from the same fogged-up lens.

But as the noise clears, both snap into focus together, and this is the crucial bit, they're dependent on each other. The clearer your destination becomes, the more obvious the path. And the better you understand the path, the more it sharpens what you were actually after in the first place. They improve in lockstep. They're in a feedback loop, pulling each other up.

So the signal isn't something you do. It's something you're already embedded in. It's more like gravity than like effort. The work isn't to generate it. The work is to get out of your own way enough to let it through.

The oldest version of this story

This is an old idea wearing new language. Read the Garden of Eden as a map of exactly this.

The tree of knowledge is what you eat from when you make your decisions out of fear and want, out of baggage. It's knowledge severed from wisdom, the act of deciding for yourself from inside the distortion. And the consequence of eating from it is the Fall, the fragmentation, the exile from direct contact with what's true. Every time we act from our trauma and our noise, we're eating from that tree again, and we're walking further from the garden. With all that garbage clouding us, getting back becomes nearly impossible, because the very faculty we'd use to find our way is the one that's been corrupted.

The tree of life is the other thing entirely. It's not knowledge grasped from fear, it's participation in the signal itself. It's operating from clarity rather than noise. Resurrection, in this reading, is the return to that, the restoration of clear sight, reunion with the truth you were cut off from. Getting closer to God and getting closer to the truth are the same motion described in two vocabularies.

Why it's collective, and why that changes everything

If this were only personal, it would be a nice theory of individual enlightenment and nothing more. But the mechanism is social, and that's what makes it inevitable rather than optional.

Watch what happens when you actually resolve some of your baggage. Say someone does the work, the real work on trauma, and comes to see the connection between their old wounds and their present reactions. You come out the other side lighter. You're a more pleasant person to be around. And here's the consequence that matters: you stop causing baggage in other people. Your fear isn't spilling onto them anymore. Your want isn't distorting how you treat them.

But it goes further than just doing no harm. A person operating with less noise tends to help the people around them clear their noise too. Healing is contagious in the same way damage is. So now there's a spiral. The more you resolve your own baggage, the less you create in others, and the more you actively help others resolve theirs, which makes them lighter, which means they create less in you and in everyone they touch.

This is why helping others isn't separate from your own evolution. It is the evolution, viewed at the collective scale. You can't reach the tree of life alone, because the signal isn't pulling individuals toward it one at a time. It's pulling the whole field at once. Your clarity raises the clarity around you, and theirs raises yours.

Why it must evolve

Put the pieces together and the conclusion is hard to avoid.

Consciousness is collective. It moves toward truth through clear experience. Baggage is the noise that blocks clear experience, and the signal of clarity is self-amplifying, less noise lets through more signal which clears more noise. That clearing is social, your purification reduces the baggage you inflict on others and actively helps resolve theirs, which feeds back into the whole.

So you get a ratchet. Every bit of genuine clearing reduces the total noise in the system and makes the next bit of clearing easier, both for you and for everyone in your orbit. The damage we do to each other goes down. And the less damage there is in the world, the better the conditions become for everyone to see clearly, which means more clearing, which means less damage still.

That's not a wish. That's the shape of the thing. Consciousness evolves because it's a self-reinforcing system tilted toward coherence, and it must evolve because every step toward clarity makes the next step more likely, individually and collectively at the same time. The world becomes a better place not because we decide it should, but because that's the direction the signal was always pointing, and we're finally, slowly, learning to stop standing in its way.
